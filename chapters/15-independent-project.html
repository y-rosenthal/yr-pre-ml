15. Independent project: advertising and sales#

In the guided project, we did every step together. Now it is your turn: you do the whole workflow alone, on new data, from reading the file to a short report for a manager. A checklist tells you what to do at each step, but not how. A model solution is at the end of the page, for when you are done.

In this chapter, you will learn to:

  • inspect, explore and clean a new data file on your own, and decide how to handle its missing values;

  • prepare X and y, train three models, and compare their errors;

  • find out which kind of advertising seems to matter, by training a model without it;

  • write a short interpretation of your results for a marketing director.

15.1. The business question#

A company sells one product in four regions. Every week, in each region, it decides how much money to spend on TV advertising and on online advertising, sets the price, and sometimes runs a promotion in the stores. Then it counts the units sold.

The marketing director asks two questions:

  1. Can we predict the units sold in a week from the advertising, the price, the promotion and the region? A good prediction helps to plan stock and budgets.

  2. Which kind of advertising seems to matter, TV or online?

15.2. The data#

The file marketing.csv has one row for each week and region, 520 rows in all (130 weeks in four regions). The appendix The datasets describes it:

  • region: North, South, East or West (text);

  • tv_spend and online_spend: the money spent on TV and online advertising that week, in thousands of dollars;

  • price: the price of the product that week, in dollars;

  • promotion: Yes or No (text): whether a store promotion ran that week;

  • units_sold: the number of units sold that week. This is the target.

15.15. Reflection questions#

15.15.1. Question 1 (Easy)#

In the chapter A first machine-learning example, the random forest predicted best. In the guided project, linear regression did. What does that teach you about choosing a model?

Answer

No kind of model is best for every problem. Which one works best depends on the data: linear regression does well when the target is roughly a sum of parts, and a forest can follow more complicated patterns. So we train several models and let the test set decide.

15.15.2. Question 2 (Medium)#

Whatever you chose in step 3, try the other way: if you dropped the rows with missing values, fill them instead (online_spend with its median, promotion with "Unknown"), or the other way round. Then compute the three MAEs again. Are the results very different?

Answer

The model solution below drops the rows. Filling instead keeps all 520 weeks, and linear regression is still the best model, with an MAE of 32.1 units (the model solution gets 28.6). A filled online_spend is only a guess, and that column matters a lot, so filling it adds some error. The test weeks are not the same in the two cases, so the numbers are not exactly comparable.

filled = marketing.copy()
filled["online_spend"] = filled["online_spend"].fillna(filled["online_spend"].median())
filled["promotion"] = filled["promotion"].fillna("Unknown")
filled = pd.get_dummies(filled, columns=["region", "promotion"], dtype=int)
X_f = filled.drop(columns="units_sold")
y_f = filled["units_sold"]
print(X_f.shape)
X_f_train, X_f_test, y_f_train, y_f_test = train_test_split(X_f, y_f, test_size=0.25, random_state=42)
models = [("linear regression", LinearRegression()),
          ("decision tree", DecisionTreeRegressor(max_depth=5, random_state=42)),
          ("random forest", RandomForestRegressor(n_estimators=200, random_state=42))]
for name, model in models:
    model.fit(X_f_train, y_f_train)
    error = mean_absolute_error(y_f_test, model.predict(X_f_test))
    print(f"{name:18} MAE: {error:.1f} units")

15.15.3. Question 3 (Hard)#

Suppose the linear model brings more extra units for each thousand dollars of online advertising than for each thousand dollars of TV advertising. Should the company move its whole TV budget to online advertising? Give two reasons to be careful.

Answer

Not at once. First, the data has no week with more than about 20 thousand dollars of online advertising (describe() shows a maximum of 19.9). The model knows nothing about much larger amounts, and the effect may get smaller as the spending grows. Second, the model found a pattern in past weeks, not a proof that one thing causes the other. And TV clearly matters too: without it, the error grows a lot. A careful step is to move a part of the TV budget for a few weeks, and to measure what happens.

Answer 
import pandas as pd
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from sklearn.model_selection import train_test_split
from sklearn.linear_model import LinearRegression
from sklearn.tree import DecisionTreeRegressor
from sklearn.ensemble import RandomForestRegressor
from sklearn.metrics import mean_absolute_error

DATA_URL = "https://raw.githubusercontent.com/y-rosenthal/yr-pre-ml/main/data/"

# Step 1: read and inspect
marketing = pd.read_csv(DATA_URL + "marketing.csv")
print(marketing.shape)
marketing.info()
print(marketing.describe().round(1))

# Step 2: explore
print(marketing["region"].value_counts())
print(marketing["promotion"].value_counts())
by_region = marketing.groupby("region")["units_sold"].mean().round(1)
print(by_region.sort_values())
print(marketing.groupby("promotion")["units_sold"].mean().round(1))
for column, cut in [("tv_spend", 25), ("online_spend", 10)]:
    high = marketing.loc[marketing[column] > cut, "units_sold"].mean()
    low = marketing.loc[marketing[column] <= cut, "units_sold"].mean()
    print(f"{column} above {cut}: {high:.1f} units; {cut} or less: {low:.1f} units")

by_region.plot(kind="bar")
plt.title("Average weekly units sold by region")
plt.xlabel("region")
plt.ylabel("average units sold")
plt.show()

# Step 3: clean. The company sets its advertising, price and promotion itself,
# so a future week has all its values. The missing values are only gaps in the
# records of past weeks, and dropping them keeps most of the weeks.
print(marketing.isna().sum())
clean = marketing.dropna()
print("weeks left:", len(clean))

# Step 4: text to numbers
clean = pd.get_dummies(clean, columns=["region", "promotion"], dtype=int)

# Step 5: X and y
X = clean.drop(columns="units_sold")
y = clean["units_sold"]
print(X.shape, y.shape)
print("average units sold per week:", round(y.mean(), 1))

# Step 6: split
X_train, X_test, y_train, y_test = train_test_split(X, y, test_size=0.25, random_state=42)
print(X_train.shape, X_test.shape)

# Steps 7 and 8: three models and their MAE
linear = LinearRegression()
linear.fit(X_train, y_train)
tree = DecisionTreeRegressor(max_depth=5, random_state=42)
tree.fit(X_train, y_train)
forest = RandomForestRegressor(n_estimators=200, random_state=42)
forest.fit(X_train, y_train)
for name, model in [("linear regression", linear), ("decision tree", tree), ("random forest", forest)]:
    error = mean_absolute_error(y_test, model.predict(X_test))
    print(f"{name:18} MAE: {error:.1f} units")

# Step 9: which advertising matters? Train the best model without each one.
for column in ["tv_spend", "online_spend"]:
    model = LinearRegression()
    model.fit(X_train.drop(columns=column), y_train)
    error = mean_absolute_error(y_test, model.predict(X_test.drop(columns=column)))
    print(f"without {column:13} MAE: {error:.1f} units")
print(pd.Series(linear.coef_, index=X.columns).round(2))

A sample interpretation, with the numbers that this code prints:

Answer

Linear regression predicts the weekly units sold best: on the test weeks, it is off by 28.6 units on average, about 7% of an average week (426.7 units), which is precise enough to plan stock. The random forest (39.1 units) and the decision tree (62.3 units) are clearly worse. Both kinds of advertising matter: without TV spending, the error of linear regression grows to 80.2 units, and without online spending to 70.6 units. For each thousand dollars, online advertising seems to bring more: 14.32 extra units a week, against 5.84 for TV. Promotions help too (466.4 units on average in promotion weeks, against 409.7), and each extra dollar of price costs about 17 units (the model’s number is -17.13). These are patterns in past weeks, not proof, so we suggest moving a part of the TV budget to online advertising for a few weeks and measuring the result.
